1Summary for the decision maker

Employee tasks are not stored facts. Every night the system throws away all task rows of a contract part and rebuilds them from contracts, locations, products, documents and activities. Only two things survive the rebuild: the previous night's state of each task (copied forward in memory) and the done / ignored markers in the table contract_part_product_done. Everything that went wrong in July and August 2026 comes from how these two carriers interact.

  • Done and Ignored are not calculated. They only ever enter the system through a user action. The recalculation can keep them or lose them; it never produces them on its own (except from a typed marker, see below).
  • Markers carry no year and, until 18 Aug 2026, no intent. A marker records today's date when the button was clicked, for a location, contract part and product. The year of the task the user acted on is not stored. Until the fix, "done" and "ignored" wrote an identical marker row; the difference lived only in the task row's state, which is deleted nightly.
  • The July release (30 Jul 2026) turned the marker into the source of truth while it still had no intent. Every task that had any marker in its year was rewritten to DONE_TASK and the old state was no longer restored. Over about 19 nightly runs, practically every ignored task in the system became a done task. This is the mechanism behind "almost all ignored tasks are missing and marked as done".
  • The August fix (18 Aug 2026) added a typed action (IGNORED / COMPLETED) to new markers and made typed markers win. Old, untyped markers are now treated as completed by the multi-year rhythm check. Because the ignored state in the task rows was already overwritten, products that were once ignored now follow the "done rhythm": the years in between are skipped and no task row is created at all. This is the mechanism behind "some entities are excluded from calculation entirely". The fix itself is assessed in 5.3 (developers) and 6.2 (owner).
  • The code cannot repair the data. The ignored intent of legacy markers exists nowhere any more except, unreliably, in the free-text reason. Any repair is a data task with the client.
  • Beyond the two incidents, the design has structural problems that will keep producing surprises: marker dated by wall-clock instead of task year (acting on a 2025 task in 2026 flips the 2026 task), a repetition guard whose inner condition is mathematically always true, past years being rewritten when a product is completed, ONCE contract parts that lose an ignored task after one year, and computed states that freeze after the first night.
  • What we need from you is the set of business rules in section 7. The most important ones: what "done" anchors the rhythm to (Q1), what "ignore for this year" should do in the following years (Q2), which year a click belongs to (Q3), and what a one-time obligation does after an ignore (Q4). A new question concerns rhythms shorter than a year (Q7): today they produce one task per year, nothing else.

4The nightly recalculation

4.1Scheduling and eligibility

DE window
@Scheduled(cron = "* * 0,1,2,3,4,5 ? * *"): every second from 00:00:00 to 05:59:59, CalculateEmployeeTasksTask.java:140. The comment says "every minute"; the expression says every second. Spring does not overlap runs of the same method, so effectively "as fast as one contract part finishes".
RS window
"* * 5 ? * *": 05:00 to 05:59, on a different thread (pool of 30, SchedulingConfigurerConfiguration.java:14). Region is a thread-local, so the overlap in hour 5 is harmless.
Per tick
Exactly one contract part: the one with the oldest lastCalculation that is null or older than 24 hours (ContractSpecification.java:15-21, ContractPartServiceImpl.java:535-539). Deleted contract parts are eligible too; they are handled inside the run.
Effect
A contract part is recalculated at most once per day, and only if the night has enough seconds for all eligible parts. New contract parts start with lastCalculation = now − 10 years (ContractPart.java:63) and are picked up the next night. Editing a contract part does not trigger a recalculation; the update copies lastCalculation unchanged (ContractPartServiceImpl.java:245).
Manual
GET /developer/employee-tasks/recalculate/contract-part/{id}?pipeline=v1. The default is v2, i.e. the inactive pipeline (DeveloperEndPoints.java:265-293).

4.2One contract part, one run

Figure 1. recalculateContractPart, CalculateEmployeeTasksTask.java:179-296.

Points that matter later:

  • All years are rebuilt every night, from the contract part's start year (possibly a decade back) up to the end year. Future years up to now+3 exist as tasks with provided amount 0.
  • End year precedence: the contract's end date wins over the contract part's own end date, even if the part ends earlier (:204-209, introduced by commit 1af9c69f8). Such rows exist but the UI hides them because contractPartActive is false.
  • Deleted contract or part: all its tasks vanish (rows and documents were already deleted), nothing is created, including history.
  • Unmatched snapshot entries are silently discarded (:294): a task that existed yesterday but is not regenerated today (location closed, customer inactive, product removed from the price list, year range shrunk, or now skipped by the multi-year guard) disappears together with its DONE_TASK / IGNORED_TASK state. Its marker, if any, survives.
  • The whole run is one database transaction; Elasticsearch writes are not transactional. A failure after ES writes leaves ES ahead of the database until the next night.

4.6Multi-year skip guard (step 1)

Purpose as written in the comment: "skip product if repetition interval is on 2 year or more and product no need to be done this year". The exact code, identical in all six methods:

int n = convertReptitionIntrvalInNumber(interval);            // 2..5, otherwise 1
if (n >= 2) {
  Optional<ContractPartProductDone> m = findDoneProduct(location, contractPart, product);
  //   newest REGULAR marker with task_action IS NULL or 'COMPLETED', ORDER BY date_done DESC LIMIT 1
  if (m.isPresent()) {
    int doneYear = m.dateDone.getYear();
    Optional<Task> rowOfDoneYear = empty;
    if (year > doneYear) rowOfDoneYear = findTopBy...Year(product, location, contractPart, doneYear);
    if (m.dateDone.isAfter(2021-10-07)
        && !(rowOfDoneYear.isPresent() && rowOfDoneYear.state == IGNORED_TASK)) {
      if ((year - doneYear) % n != 0 && product.id != 28) {
        if (isTaskDone(m) || year != doneYear + n)   // always true here, see below
          continue;                                   // skip this year entirely
      }
    }
  }
}

What it does in practice, case by case. "Legacy" below means a marker row written before 18 August 2026, whose task_action is NULL because the column did not exist yet:

Newest completed / legacy markerTask row of the marker yearResult for other years
None (or only typed IGNORED markers, which the query excludes)—No skipping. A task exists for every year in range; the state of each year comes from its own marker or the computation.
Typed COMPLETED, dated year DDONE_TASK (by the marker)Every year Y with (Y − D) mod n ≠ 0 is skipped: no row, no document. This includes years before D, because Java's remainder of a negative number is non-zero unless it divides exactly. Anniversary years D ± n, D ± 2n … get a normal computed task.
Legacy untyped, dated DIGNORED_TASK (survived)Guard bypassed completely: a task is generated every year, indefinitely, not only "next year". Stops only when a newer completed or legacy marker appears.
Legacy untyped, dated Danything else (OPEN, DONE_TASK, …)Treated exactly like a completion in D: non-anniversary years skipped. After the July release rewrote ignored rows to DONE_TASK, this is the case for almost all formerly ignored products.
Any marker dated on or before 2021-10-07—Guard never skips (hard-coded cut-over, :77).
Any, product id 28 ("fire helper")—Never skipped (hard-coded literal 28, :344).

The inner condition is dead code. isTaskDone(m) || year != doneYear + n is reached only when (year − doneYear) % n ≠ 0. If year == doneYear + n the remainder would be 0 and the block would not be entered. So whenever the line executes, year != doneYear + n is already true and the continue always fires. The result of isTaskDone, and the intent behind it ("only skip if the marker really meant done"), has no influence on the outcome. Verified arithmetically, confirmed by the colleague's initial-state analysis.

Only the newest marker counts. LIMIT 1 means a product done in 2023 and again in 2024 (interval 2) is judged from 2024 only: the 2023 row is (2023 − 2024) mod 2 = −1 → skipped, and the 2023 DONE_TASK disappears from history the next night.

4.7Computed states (step 3)

Two overloads, :972-1005, plus the marker translation :1007-1014:

markerState = marker == null || marker.action == null ? null
            : marker.action == IGNORED ? IGNORED_TASK : DONE_TASK;

calculateState(marker, fulfilled)                  // FlatRate, MultipleTimes, TimeTracking, Dummy, DummyOnce
  = markerState ?: (fulfilled ? FULFILLED : OPEN)

calculateState(marker, fulfilled, neededThisYear)  // NotEveryYear, PerPiece
  = markerState ?: (!neededThisYear ? NOT_THIS_YEAR : fulfilled ? FULFILLED : OPEN)

marker here is the REGULAR marker whose dateDone lies inside the task's year (ContractPartProductDoneServiceImpl.java:63-65), looked up per location × contract part × product × year. This is the only year-aware marker use in the whole pipeline, and it works only because dateDone happens to be in the right year when the user acts in the same year as the task.

"Needed this year" for the two multi-year document methods (:1043-1077): take the year of the newest document of the product's document type on the location (if the product has no document type, or no document exists: contract part start year minus the interval factor). The product is due if year == lastDocYear or year − lastDocYear − factor ≥ 0. Example with factor 2 and last document 2024: 2025 not due (NOT_THIS_YEAR, calculated amount 0), 2026 due, 2027 due, 2028 due. Note that this rhythm is driven by documents, while the skip guard in step 1 is driven by markers. The two mechanisms are independent and can disagree: a task marked done without a document still counts as "due" every year afterwards until the guard removes the row.

The "fire helper" special case (:537-543) is meant to use sent fire-protection certificates instead of location documents for product 28. It compares FIRE_HELPER_PRODUCT_ID, a Long, with product.getDocumentType(), an entity (Product.java:43-45). That is never equal, so the block never runs. This settles the question the colleague left open: it is a real defect, not an id-space coincidence.

4.8Restoring yesterday's state (step 4)

for (oldTask : snapshot)
  if (oldTask matches year, product, contractPart, location)   // responsible user deliberately ignored
    if (!(task.state is DONE_TASK or IGNORED_TASK)) task.state = oldTask.state;
    task.seen = sameResponsible ? oldTask.seen : false;
    remove oldTask from snapshot; break;

Reading: a typed marker wins; otherwise yesterday always wins. Two consequences:

  • User-set DONE_TASK / IGNORED_TASK survive nightly rebuilds even when no marker exists for that year (this is how pre-2026 behaviour worked, and how a marker dated in another year still leaves the clicked row correct).
  • Computed states are frozen after the first night a row exists. If a future year was created as OPEN (no documents yet) and a document arrives later, the recomputed FULFILLED or NOT_THIS_YEAR is immediately replaced by yesterday's OPEN. Because the UI does not distinguish these four states, nobody sees it, but state cannot be trusted as a measure of fulfilment, and rows still carrying the legacy DONE (id 3) stay that way forever and count as open.

4.9ONCE contract parts

Contract parts with repetition interval ONCE take a separate path (:211-220, 821-905). For each location × product one marker is read once, not year-scoped: findFirstByEntityStatusAndCustomerLocationAndContractPartAndProduct(REGULAR, …) without any ordering (ContractPartProductDoneRepository.java:26-28). Then for every year in range:

SituationResult
Marker exists and year == marker.dateDone.yearOne task with state = typed marker state, or OPEN for an untyped marker, then step 4 restore. If the resulting state is IGNORED_TASK, a second task for year + 1 is created with state OPEN, without the restore step (:855-871).
Marker exists, other yearsNothing. No task row for any other year, including the current one.
No marker, year == current year (or the part has ended and year == end year)One OPEN task, then restore.
No marker, other yearsNothing.

So a ONCE obligation shows exactly one open task (current year) until somebody acts. After "Done" it shows one done task in the year of the click, forever. After "Ignore for this year" (year I) it shows the ignored task in I and an OPEN task in I + 1. In I + 2 there is no task for the current year at all: the obligation has silently disappeared. If the employee marks the I + 1 task done, a second marker (COMPLETED, year I + 1) is created; the next night the unordered findFirst picks one of the two markers, in practice the older IGNORED one, and rebuilds I + 1 as OPEN, losing the completion.

4.10Elasticsearch and what the UI shows

  • Each saved row is mirrored to one document (:1121-1215). openTask = state ∉ {DONE_TASK, IGNORED_TASK}; doneReason = reason of this year's marker (for ONCE: of the single marker); contractPartActive / contractActive = whether the year overlaps the part's / contract's validity.
  • The task page and AMS section list documents grouped by location → contract part → product; the year filter is mandatory and defaults to the current year; "open" means NOT (DONE_TASK OR IGNORED_TASK); "done" and "ignored" are exact state matches (CalculatedContractTaskProductServiceImpl.java:1006-1012). Only documents with contractPartActive and contractActive true are shown.
  • Counts (open / done / ignored) are document counts with the same filters (:663-677). Customer progress marks a location "done" if all its documents are DONE_TASK or IGNORED_TASK, "ignored" if all are IGNORED_TASK, otherwise "open" (:150-166).
  • "Ignored task date" is not a separate field. For a task whose state is IGNORED_TASK, and only then (CalculatedContractTaskProductServiceImpl.java:704-705), the list reads dateDone of the newest REGULAR marker of that location × part × product in the task's year with action null or IGNORED (ContractPartProductDoneRepository.java:35-38) and shows it under the label "Ignored task date". Tasks in state DONE_TASK show no date at all; there is no "done date" column. The value is simply the day the employee clicked.
  • The "Done" confirmation text tells the employee: "If you set this task as done, the rhythm according to the contract will be applied. This task will then be displayed / due again in 1, 2, … years." This sentence is the closest thing to a specification of the intended rhythm behaviour that exists in the repository.

4.11Intervals shorter than a year

The repetition interval table contains TWO_WEEKLY (factor 26), MONTHLY (12), QUARTERLY (4), SEMESTRAL (2) and YEARLY (1), each flagged less_than_year (V2018_08_08_121716__HSC-9-contract-part-and-enums.sql). The pipeline never reads that flag and never creates more than one task per year for a location × product, whatever the interval. The factor is used in exactly one place:

  • DOCUMENT_BASED with FLAT_RATE or PER_LOCATION (calculateDocumentBasedMultipleTimesTask, :414, 445-448): the yearly target is factor × hours per location, so a monthly part expects 12 × hours in the year, and "provided" is the number of documents of the product's document type in that year × hours. Fulfilment is judged once for the whole year and, as section 4.8 explains, the FULFILLED state is frozen and invisible anyway. The employee sees one task with one target amount, not twelve tasks.
  • Every other combination with a sub-yearly interval falls into calculateDummyTask (section 4.3): FLAT_RATE with anything but YEARLY, TIME_AND_MATERIAL with anything but YEARLY (or the multi-year list for PER_PIECE). Those tasks have all amounts 0 and a state that is OPEN until a user acts. The interval has no effect at all.
  • "Done" and "Ignore for this year" apply to the whole year in both cases. There is no notion of "done for March" or "done for Q2".

So the answer to "how are weekly and monthly tasks calculated" is: they are not. They are yearly tasks whose target amount is multiplied by the factor in one billing combination and ignored in all others. Whether that is the intended product behaviour is asked in Q7 (section 7).

5.2What happened between 30 July and 18 August

  1. Before the release, an ignored task was a row with state IGNORED_TASK plus an untyped marker dated the day of the click. Every done task looked identical in the marker table.
  2. The release made "there is a marker in this year" mean DONE_TASK and stopped restoring the old state for such rows. On the first nightly run after deployment, every ignored task of every recalculated contract part became DONE_TASK, in the database and in Elasticsearch. Since each contract part is recalculated daily, the whole system was converted within days.
  3. Side effect on multi-year parts: the guard's bypass condition reads the row of the marker year. As soon as that row said DONE_TASK, the guard treated the product as completed and skipped the non-anniversary years. Rows for those years were deleted, together with any state they carried, and unmatched snapshot entries were dropped.
  4. The ONCE branch hard-coded DONE_TASK for the marker year and skipped the restore, so ignored ONCE tasks were converted as well and their "next year" OPEN task stopped being created.
  5. The fix restored the ability to distinguish the two actions for new markers only. For legacy markers it deliberately does not guess (comment in ContractPartProductDone.java:33-36), so the computed state applies and the restore copies forward what the rows say now: DONE_TASK. The guard additionally counts untyped markers as completions. Net effect for a legacy-ignored product: it stays "done" in its year and vanishes from the following non-anniversary years.

Data is out of scope for this audit, but it must be said plainly: after step 2 there is no field in the database that records that a task was ignored rather than done. The reason text is a hint at best. In the UI, "done" tasks never had a reason field, "ignore for this year" offered one (optional, not enforced), the map dialog stores its text in the same column, and the 2022 HSC-Itas script wrote a reason on done markers. The "ignored task date" column cannot help either: it is shown only for tasks that still carry the IGNORED_TASK state, so the converted tasks show no date at all.

5.3Assessment of the 18 August fix (PR #2412)

What the fix consists of, mechanically:

  1. ContractPartProductDoneAction { IGNORED, COMPLETED } and a nullable column task_action; the migration leaves all existing rows NULL on purpose (V2026_08_13_100000).
  2. getExplicitMarkerState(marker) (CalculateEmployeeTasksTask.java:1007-1014): typed IGNORED → IGNORED_TASK, typed COMPLETED → DONE_TASK, no marker or untyped → null, which makes calculateState fall through to the computed state.
  3. isExplicitMarkerState(state) (:1016-1018) guards the restore loop in all six methods and in calculateDummyTaskOnce: yesterday's state is copied only when the new state is not DONE_TASK / IGNORED_TASK. Since computed states never produce those two values, the rule is exactly "typed marker of this year wins, otherwise yesterday wins".
  4. isTaskDone (:1233-1243) answers from the action when typed and falls back to the snapshot for untyped rows. As shown in 4.6 its result is never used.
  5. findDoneProduct now excludes typed IGNORED markers; getIgnoredTaskDate accepts NULL or IGNORED (ContractPartProductDoneRepository.java:30-38).
  6. changeState validates the requested state and passes the action into create() (CalculatedContractTaskProductServiceImpl.java:493-497, 523-525).
  7. create() (ContractPartProductDoneServiceImpl.java:83-180) requires an action, keys everything to the current calendar year, replaces a current-year marker whose action differs, keeps one whose action is the same (without updating the reason), syncs the current-year task in DB and ES, and for the batch path refuses to overwrite an ignore.
  8. Frontend callers send the action: the ignore dialog sends IGNORED, the task page's single "done" and the appointment popup send COMPLETED. V2 received the same changes (CalculateEmployeeTasksV2Task.java:752-797).

What is right. Intent is finally recorded for every new action; the precedence between marker and snapshot is explicit and applied consistently in all copies; the appointment popup cannot override a deliberate ignore; the endpoint no longer accepts arbitrary states; refusing to guess legacy intent from reason is defensible given the counter-examples in the code.

What it does not solve, and what it changed for the worse.

  • Legacy rows are frozen in their corrupted state. For an untyped marker the state is computed and then overwritten by yesterday's DONE_TASK. The comment in create() ("cannot change an ignored marker into a completed task anymore") holds for typed markers only.
  • Legacy rows count as completions for the rhythm (task_action is null or 'COMPLETED'), while the bypass still depends on the marker-year row saying IGNORED_TASK. This is the direct cause of the "excluded entities" report (F10).
  • The current-year sync now propagates IGNORED as well as DONE. Before the fix an action on another year's task could only flip the current-year task to done; now it can also hide it (F1).
  • Typed IGNORED markers are invisible to the guard. Before the fix the newest marker, whatever it meant, was found and the bypass could trigger; now an ignore after a completion never influences the rhythm (F9). This is a behavioural change that nobody asked for and that contradicts the button label.
  • The tautology, the negative-modulo history rewrite, LIMIT 1, the ONCE lookup without ordering and the frozen computed states are untouched (F7, F8, F15, F16, F3).
  • Replacing an untyped marker discards its reason text and with it the last hint of the original intent (F6).

Verdict for developers. Keep the typed action and the precedence rule; they are the foundation for any correct implementation. Do not build further on the guard: it needs to be rewritten from the answers to Q1, Q2 and Q3, with the marker carrying the task year explicitly, and the six copies must be collapsed into one method first.

6What goes wrong, in business terms

Sections 6 and 7 are written for the product owner and can be read on their own. They repeat the essentials of sections 1 to 5 without code. The small grey references such as F4 point to the developer findings in section 8.

6.1Four rules the system follows today

  1. A task is one product, at one location, for one calendar year. There is never more than one task per year for the same product and location, also when the contract says every two weeks, monthly or quarterly. "Done" and "Ignore for this year" always apply to the whole year.
  2. Every night all tasks of a contract part are thrown away and built again from the contract, its locations and products, the documents and the recorded activities. Only two things are carried across the night: the Done or Ignored status each task had yesterday, and the notes.
  3. A note is the record written when somebody clicks Done or Ignore (the developer sections call it a marker). It stores the location, the contract part, the product, the day of the click, an optional reason text and, since 18 August 2026, whether the click was Done or Ignore. Notes written before that date do not say which of the two it was. A note never stores which year's task was clicked; the system assumes the year of the click.
  4. For products with a 2 to 5 year rhythm, notes decide in which years a task exists at all. A year the system considers "not in the rhythm" simply has no task: it is not shown as done or hidden, it is absent from every list, count and progress bar.

Figure 4. The nightly rebuild in plain words. Figures 1 to 3 in section 4 show the same thing in full detail.

6.2The 18 August fix: what it repaired and what it did not

The fix introduced the note type (Done or Ignore) and one clear rule: a typed note for a year decides the status of that year's task, and yesterday's status is used only when there is no typed note. In addition, the popup after an appointment no longer marks a product done when it is currently ignored, and the "change status" request accepts only Open, Done and Ignored.

Repaired

  • Every new Done or Ignore is recorded with its type. From 18 August on, a nightly rebuild can no longer turn an ignored task into a done one.
  • The rule "typed note wins, otherwise yesterday's status" is explicit and applied consistently in all six calculation variants and in the one-time variant.
  • An appointment cannot silently override a deliberate Ignore of the same year.

Not repaired

  • Old notes. Notes written before 18 August still have no type. The fix deliberately does not guess, so their tasks keep whatever status they have now, which for formerly ignored tasks is "Done" (see problem 1). For the rhythm, old notes count as Done (problem 2).
  • The day of the click still stands in for the year of the task. A Done or Ignore on last year's task creates a note in this year and changes this year's task as well (problem 3). The fix made that side effect stronger: it now also happens for Ignore, not only for Done.
  • The rhythm logic itself was not touched. Everything in problems 4, 5 and 6 is exactly as before, plus one new twist: a typed Ignore is invisible to the rhythm, so an ignore after an earlier Done does not bring the task back next year.

Verdict. The core rule of the fix is the right rule and it stops the bleeding for everything employees do from now on, as long as they act in the same year as the task. It is not a complete solution: the damaged data stays damaged, the click-day problem stays, and the rhythm rules still need to be defined and rebuilt. Details: section 4.7, 4.8, 5, F1, F2, F6, F9, F10

6.3The problems, one by one

Problem 1

Ignored tasks became done tasks (30 July to 18 August 2026)

What you see
Tasks that employees had hidden with "Ignore for this year" are listed under Done. Their reason text is still there, but the status says done. The "ignored task date" column is empty for them, because it is only shown for tasks whose status is still Ignored.
Why
The July release made "there is a note for this year" mean Done, and stopped keeping yesterday's status for such tasks. Every Ignore had written a note, so every ignored task was rewritten to Done during the first nightly rebuild after the release. Because each contract part is rebuilt daily, the whole system was converted within days.
Can code fix it?
No. The only place where "ignored" was stored was the task status, and that was overwritten. The notes never said whether they meant Done or Ignore. Repair is a data task: see question 8.

The guard's inner condition is always true

Medium
Where
CalculateEmployeeTasksTask.java:345 and five copies; isTaskDone :1233-1243
Effect
Any non-anniversary year is skipped as soon as the guard is active. The check that was supposed to make the skip depend on "the marker really meant done" has no effect; isTaskDone and its snapshot lookup are dead code. Section 4.6 has the proof.
F8

Completing a product rewrites past years

Medium
Where
:344, remainder of a negative difference
Effect
With interval n and completion year D, past years Y where (Y − D) is not a multiple of n lose their task row the next night, including any DONE_TASK / IGNORED_TASK they carried. History and past-year progress figures change retroactively.
F9

Only the newest completion counts; an ignore after a completion follows the old rhythm

MediumDecision
Where
ContractPartProductDoneRepository.java:30-33 (LIMIT 1, typed IGNORED excluded)
Effect
Typed IGNORED markers are invisible to the guard. If a product was completed in D and ignored at the anniversary D + n, the next task appears at D + 2n, not the year after the ignore. If no completion exists at all, an ignored product reappears next year and every year after. Neither matches the UI wording "ignore for this year" unambiguously.
F10

Legacy markers count as completions

High
Where
ContractPartProductDoneRepository.java:31 (task_action is null or 'COMPLETED') combined with the bypass at :342-343
Effect
For a legacy ignore the guard is bypassed only if the row of the marker year still says IGNORED_TASK. After July almost none do, so formerly ignored products behave as completed: non-anniversary years get no task at all. This is the mechanism behind "entities excluded from calculation".

F14

Two independent notions of "due this year"

MediumDecision
Where
Documents: :1043-1077; markers: :332-350
Effect
A document shifts the due-year rhythm (NOT_THIS_YEAR, calculated amount 0, row still exists and counts as open). A marker removes rows entirely. A done click without a document leaves later years "due" by the document rule and "skipped" by the marker rule at the same time. Which rule is authoritative has never been defined.

F15

An ignored ONCE task reappears for one year, then vanishes

High
Where
:832-875
Effect
Only year + 1 is created, and only while the ignored marker's year is processed. From year + 2 on, the current year has no task. The year + 1 row skips the restore step, so completing it is undone every night once a second marker exists (F5, F16).

ONCE reads one arbitrary marker across all years

Medium
Where

Effect
With an IGNORED marker in year I and a COMPLETED marker in I + 1, one of the two years loses its task depending on which row the database returns first.

ONCE parts have no history and no current-year task once any marker exists

LowDecision
Effect
Without a marker only the current year has a row; with a marker only the marker year has one. Whether an obligation "once per contract" should keep showing as done in later years is a product question.
